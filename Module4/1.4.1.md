A – Arrived Clients Count

A tells us how many patients arrive at the health centre during a specific period of time.

For example, if 100 patients arrive at the health centre during one day, A = 100. This helps us understand the number of patients and the demand for healthcare services.

C – Clients Serviced Count

C tells us how many patients have completed their service.

For example, if 90 patients have visited a doctor during one day, C = 90. This helps us evaluate how many patients the health centre is able to serve.

B – Busy Time

B tells us how long a service point has been actively working. In other words, it measures how much time a doctor or nurse spends serving patients.

For example, if a doctor spends six hours seeing patients during a working day, B = 360 minutes.

T – Total Simulation Time

T means the total length of the period being observed.

For example, if the health centre is observed during an eight-hour working day, T = 480 minutes.

U – Utilization

U tells us how much of the available time a service point is busy serving patients.

Formula:

U = B / T

For example, if a doctor spends 360 minutes with patients during an eight-hour working day, the utilization is 75%. This means that the doctor is busy 75% of the observed time.

X – Throughput

X tells us how many patients a service point can serve within a certain period of time.

Formula:

X = C / T

For example, if a doctor serves 90 patients in 480 minutes, the throughput is 11.25 patients per hour. This helps us evaluate the efficiency of the service point.

S – Average Service Time

S tells us how long it takes to serve one patient on average. This means the actual service time, not the waiting time.

Formula:

S = B / C

For example, if a doctor spends 360 minutes serving 90 patients, the average service time is 4 minutes per patient.

Rᵢ – Individual Response Time

Rᵢ tells us how much time an individual patient spends in the system, from arrival until the service is completed.

It includes both waiting time and actual service time.

For example, if a patient waits 15 minutes to see a doctor and the appointment takes 5 minutes, the patient's response time is 20 minutes.

W – Cumulative Response Time

W means the total sum of the response times of all patients who have been served.

For example, if 90 patients have an average response time of 20 minutes, the cumulative response time is 1,800 minutes.

R – Average Response Time

R tells us how much time a patient spends in the healthcare service system on average.

Formula:

R = W / C

For example, if the total response time of 90 patients is 1,800 minutes, the average response time is 20 minutes.

This variable helps us evaluate how quickly patients receive the services they need.

N – Average Number of Clients in the System

N tells us how many patients are in the health centre system on average. This includes both patients waiting in the queue and patients currently receiving treatment.

Formula:

N = W / T

For example, if the cumulative response time is 1,800 minutes and the observation time is 480 minutes, there are on average 3.75 patients in the system.
